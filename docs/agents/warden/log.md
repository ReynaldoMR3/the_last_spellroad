### 2026-07-21 -- HP/damage design-intent proposal (pre-template, dispatched by Ana ahead of Pato)

Not a wave/boss composition -- a design-intent proposal against the newly locked HP/death conceptual design (closing the GDD's top BLOCKING finding). Pato owns the actual HP pool size, per-hit damage numbers, and debuff magnitudes; this entry records only the pacing-informed *intent* Warden handed off for Pato to finalize into the numeric template, per the same author/grader split that governs wave composition.

Proposed, framed as % of whatever base HP pool Pato sets (not absolute numbers):

1. Regular wave total-damage-threat: ~10-15% of HP pool for a positioning-competent player, ~25-35% for a careless one. Consistent with HP resetting every wave (unlike Mana, which persists across an expedition) -- this is a per-wave budget, not cumulative.
2. Boss/trial total-damage-threat: careful play cumulative ~40-60% of pool by the end of the fight; careless play should threaten 70-90%+ (real death risk), mirroring how Mana pressure "catches up" in trials -- but with zero in-combat recovery instead of Mana's steady 5/sec, so the threat curve is steeper per unit of misplay.
3. Per-hit starting ranges (rough, all as % of base pool, Pato to convert to absolute numbers once pool size is set): Melee contact ~5-8% per hit (higher -- melee proximity is the more avoidable-through-positioning damage source, so it should punish bad positioning harder). Ranged ~3-5% per hit (lower per-hit, but harder to fully avoid, so it supplies more of the steady pressure in a long trial).
4. Debuffer magnitude recommendations: speed drain ~10-15% move-speed reduction per application; Mana-regen drain ~1-2 Mana/sec reduction per application (off the base 5/sec). Flagging a stacking risk on both: multiplicative or uncapped-stack drain can spiral (speed toward zero breaks positioning-based play entirely; regen toward zero turns "careful budgeting" into "hard lockout"). Recommend Pato treat both as additive with a hard stack cap (2-3) and a regen floor above zero, not multiplicative/uncapped.
5. Pacing risk flagged, not solved: "no regen in combat" + a genuinely long multi-phase boss/trial means a single bad early phase could compound into an unrecoverable death spiral regardless of how well the player plays the rest of the fight -- turning the trial into "one mistake locks in a loss" rather than a running skill/budget test. Whether phase transitions get any partial HP recovery, or whether this is accepted as intended stakes, is a developer/Ana call. Secondary risk: since Debuffer's speed-vs-regen drain is a per-instance variety knob Warden picks when composing waves, a wave/trial with multiple Debuffers on the *same* drain type could stack beyond whatever safe cap Pato sets -- Warden will need to watch that mix once actual wave composition against this template begins.

### 2026-07-21 -- First real wave/boss composition: the vertical slice's mini-boss/Director trial (pending Pato gate-check)

First actual `wave.json`-schema entry Warden has produced -- no prior file existed in the repo; the GDD's `warden_hound` example above is illustrative only (invented enemy name, not one of the three base types) and was never a shipped entry. This composition targets the vertical slice's one mini-boss/Director avatar (GDD, "Seven-Week Vertical Slice" -- exactly one ships), designed against Pato's now-finalized `hp-template.md`, specifically the Phase-Transition Partial HP Recovery cap formula and 33% money ceiling.

**Design decision -- phase-break count:** 2 phase-breaks (3 phases), not more. Reasoning: the cap formula gives `min(phase_breaks - 1, 3)` recoveries, so 2 breaks caps at 1 recovery -- and the money ceiling (33% of competent-play budget) can never clear a 2nd recovery (30 HP restored needs a ~91 HP budget, above the entire stated 40-60 HP boss/trial competent-play range) regardless of phase count. Adding a 3rd or 4th phase-break would only buy cap-formula headroom the money ceiling can never actually use at this budget scale -- padding phase count for its own sake, not for pacing. 3 phases is still a genuinely multi-phase, escalating fight (Spam-Waves-Vs.-Tactical-Trials' "long, higher-HP, possibly multi-phase" target), just without wasted, never-reachable recovery slots.

**Threat budget targeted:** competent-play cumulative 51 HP (51% of the 100-point pool) -- deliberately upper-middle of the stated 40-60% band, not the floor, specifically to clear the money ceiling with margin rather than skate its edge. Careless-play cumulative ~79 HP (79%, within the 70-90%+ band -- real death risk, not certain death).

**Composition (aggregate across 3 phases):** Melee x5 (7 dmg/hit), Ranged x11 (4 dmg/hit), Debuffer x3 (one per phase, drain type varied per phase -- speed / mana-regen / speed -- specifically so no two concurrent Debuffers in the same phase share a drain type, closing the stacking risk flagged in the entry above). Model used: careless play = every enemy's fixed per-hit value landing once (5x7 + 11x4 = 35 + 44 = 79 HP); competent play = Ranged fully realized (hard to avoid per template) + only ~20% of Melee realized (the more positioning-avoidable source) = 11x4 + 0.2x5x7 = 44 + 7 = 51 HP. Debuffer contributes 0 direct HP damage in both, per template.

**Money-ceiling check:** cap formula allows 1 recovery (2 breaks - 1 = 1). Ceiling allows up to 33% x 51 = 16.83 HP recoverable; one 15-HP recovery = 15 HP = 29.4% of the 51-HP budget -- clears the 33% ceiling with ~3.6-point margin. Both constraints agree: **1 recovery, offered at either phase-break, only 1 total purchase across the whole fight** -- not 2, even though there are 2 break opportunities.

**hp_modifier / damage_modifier held at 1.0 (not tuned up) for a specific, checkable reason:** no template defines a base enemy/boss HP number for Warden to scale against (`hp-template.md` only fixes the *player's* pool and the *per-hit damage the player takes*, never how much HP an enemy itself has), and the per-hit damage values are the fixed numbers themselves, not a base to multiply -- inventing a multiplier on either would be inventing an ungrounded number, which Warden does not do. The "long, higher-HP" pacing target is instead delivered entirely through phase count, composition, and spawn cadence, all checkable against the fixed per-hit table above. Flagging for Pato/developer: if a future boss wants enemies that are individually tougher (not just more numerous), that needs a base enemy-HP template field that doesn't exist yet -- not something Warden should approximate now.

Full JSON entry, arithmetic, and phase breakdown handed to Ana in this session's report. **This composition has not been validated by Pato and must not be treated as ship-ready until Pato runs the cap-formula and money-ceiling gate-check against it** -- per the author/grader split, Warden cannot self-validate its own numeric submission.

Ceiling = 35% x 51 HP budget = 17.85 HP recoverable. One 10-HP recovery = 10 HP = 19.6% of the 51-HP budget -- clears the 35% ceiling with 7.85 HP of margin (previously 3.6 HP of margin under the old 33% ceiling at 15 HP -- more headroom under the new numbers, not less). Cap formula is unaffected by this composition's phase-break count (2 breaks - 1 = 1, unchanged whether the outer cap is 2 or 3). **Conclusion unchanged: 1 recovery, offered at either phase-break, only 1 total purchase across the whole fight.** This composition does not need re-tuning -- only the HP amount a purchased recovery restores changes (10 HP instead of 15 HP), not whether one is available.

### 2026-07-23 -- First regular-wave batch: Level 1, waves 0-2 (pending Pato gate-check)

First regular-wave (non-boss) `wave.json` entries Warden has produced -- prior entries in this log are all boss/trial (the mini-boss composition) or pre-template design intent. Dispatched by Ana for backlog Task 2.4: 2-3 waves for Level 1, the very first level the player plays.

**Composition strategy:** rather than treat "first level" as license to skip the damage-threat budget, I held all three waves to the same regular-wave band (`hp-template.md`, 10-15% competent / 25-35% careless) using the same modeling convention the mini-boss entry established -- careless play = every enemy's fixed per-hit value landing once; competent play = Ranged fully realized (hard to avoid per template) + ~20% of Melee realized (the more positioning-avoidable source). Solving that model across the stated bands narrows the viable Melee/Ranged count pairs sharply: a pure-Melee wave can't clear the competent floor at any count without blowing the careless ceiling (Melee x4 = 28 careless / 5.6 competent, too low; Melee x8 clears competent at 11.2 but careless hits 56%, nearly double the ceiling), and Ranged alone caps out fast since Ranged counts fully in both models (Ranged x4 alone already puts competent at 16, over the 15% ceiling before a single Melee is added). Only two clean integer solutions exist under this exact convention: Melee3/Ranged2 (29% careless / 12.2% competent) and Melee2/Ranged3 (26% careless / 14.8% competent). I used both, then built the third wave by reusing the Melee3/Ranged2 numbers and layering in two Debuffers of different variants -- since Debuffers add 0 direct HP under the fixed per-hit table, this keeps the same checkable budget while still escalating the *encounter*, not just repeating it.

**Wave 0 -- Melee-primary intro, light Ranged accent:** spellbound_thug x3 (Melee), hexbow_skirmisher x2 (Ranged). Careless 3x7 + 2x4 = 29 HP (29%). Competent 2x4 + 0.2x3x7 = 12.2 HP (12.2%). Both inside band.

**Wave 1 -- Ranged-primary, Debuffer introduced:** spellbound_thug x2, hexbow_skirmisher x3, murmur_wisp x1 (mana-regen-drain variant). Careless 2x7 + 3x4 = 26 HP (26%). Competent 3x4 + 0.2x2x7 = 14.8 HP (14.8%, near the band's top -- flagging this as the tightest margin of the three, no further Ranged should be added to this composition without re-checking against the 15% ceiling). One mana-regen-drain application: 5/sec base - 1.5 = 3.5/sec, above the 2/sec floor, one application under the 2-application cap.

**Wave 2 -- Level-1 send-off, dual-variant Debuffer:** spellbound_thug x3, hexbow_skirmisher x2, creeping_bramble x1 (speed-drain), murmur_wisp x1 (mana-regen-drain). Careless and competent arithmetic identical to Wave 0 (29% / 12.2%) since Debuffers contribute 0 direct HP -- deliberate, not an oversight: this wave's design job is to test the Debuffer mechanic against an already-validated damage budget, not to introduce a new one. Used two *different* drain variants (one speed, one regen) specifically so no two concurrent Debuffers share a drain type, closing the stacking risk flagged in the 2026-07-21 pre-template entry -- each sits at 1 application, well under its own 2-application cap.

**hp_modifier / damage_modifier held at 1.0 across all three,** same reasoning as the mini-boss entry: no template defines a base enemy-HP number to scale against, and the per-hit values are the fixed numbers themselves, not a multiplier base.

Spawn timing (not template-fixed, Warden's own compositional choice): each wave staggers its groups across roughly 0-2.4s from wave start, Melee first, Ranged shortly after, Debuffer(s) last -- consistent with the "resolve quickly" regular-wave pacing target, giving the player time to react to each new pressure type without any single wave dragging into Mana-management territory.

**This batch has not been validated by Pato and must not be treated as ship-ready** until Pato runs the per-hit-table and damage-threat-budget check against it, per the author/grader split -- Warden cannot self-validate its own numeric submission. Flagging for Pato specifically: Wave 1's 14.8% competent-play figure sits close enough to the 15% ceiling that it's worth an explicit pass/fail call rather than an assumed pass.

### 2026-07-25 -- Ranged/debuffer wall-pin resolution (roster consultation response, blocks backlog 2.10)

Dispatched by Ana following the 3-agent consultation (Heckler + Warden + Ana, logged `ana/log.md` 2026-07-25 (2)) that surfaced this as a real problem, not just a hypothetical: once 2.10 clamps enemies to the lane rectangle, ranged (pref. range 220) and debuffer (pref. range 200) kiting AI (`src/entities/Enemy.ts` `update()`) backs away to hold distance and can get pinned against the lane's short ends and stack on each other. Numbers grounding this: `ROAD_WIDTH` 780px, `ROAD_HEIGHT` 160px (`src/scenes/SpellroadScene.ts`), melee range 34, ranged 220, debuffer 200 (`Enemy.ts`) -- and `level-1.json` waves 1-2 already pair ranged (`hexbow_skirmisher`) with debuffer (`murmur_wisp`) in the same encounter, so this isn't a future-hypothetical wave, it ships day one.

**Resolution picked: combination (option 3) -- both a numeric retune and a behavior spec, because either alone leaves a gap:**

1. **Numeric retune:** `DEBUFFER_PREFERRED_RANGE` 200 -> **150**; `RANGED_PREFERRED_RANGE` 220 -> **240**. Widens the ring gap from 20px to **90px** -- roughly 3.5x the enemy sprite's own footprint (26x26px, `Enemy.ts` `fillRoundedRect`), so the two archetypes settle at visibly distinct distance bands instead of nearly the same ring. `MELEE_RANGE` (34) and all other archetype constants untouched.
2. **Behavior spec (Loomwright's to implement, this is design intent not code):** when a kiting enemy's retreat vector would be clamped by the lane rectangle bounds (i.e., straight-line retreat is blocked), blend in a perpendicular component (rotate the retreat vector 90 deg, scaled by remaining lane-height headroom off centerline, capped by `ROAD_HEIGHT`'s +/-80px half-span) so the enemy slides along the wall rather than pinning nose-first into it.

**Why neither alone suffices:** a pure range retune can't structurally eliminate wall-pinning -- the lane is finite (780px) and the player can always crowd either short end, at which point *any* fixed preferred range gets clamped regardless of its value; that's a positioning problem, not a tuning problem, hence the behavior spec. Conversely, a pure behavior fix without widening the range gap would still funnel ranged and debuffer into a near-identical 20px-wide ring at the same wall segment -- with only 160px of lane height to slide within, that's not enough room to visually separate two archetypes crowding the same band; hence the range retune carries its share of the fix instead of leaning entirely on steering code to paper over a numbers problem.

**Confidence / Pato gate:** I am not treating the retuned preferred-range numbers as self-cleared. `hp-template.md` fixes HP, per-hit damage, and debuff magnitude -- it has no entry for kiting-AI preferred-range values, so this is new numeric territory outside the tables I can already check my own work against (unlike the wave-budget arithmetic above, which I can self-verify against a fixed table before Pato ever sees it). Per the standing author/grader split, I want **Pato's independent check** on the 150/240 pair (and on whether the 90px gap is the right target, not just "wider than 20") before Loomwright implements either half of this.
