## 2026-07-21 — HP/Hexcoin mechanic bundle critique (dispatched by Ana)

Critiqued the new HP pool (100, no in-combat regen, reset per wave/checkpoint), the three enemy archetypes (Melee 7/Ranged 4/Debuffer speed-or-Manaregen drain), and the Hexcoin-gated phase-transition HP recovery (15%/phase-break, fee = 50% of [BASIS FLAGGED]) against `hp-template.md` and `hexcoin-template.md`.

- **Systems designer:** BLOCKING — paying Hexcoin to recover up to 15%/phase-break (stacking across multiple phase-breaks in one boss fight) lets currency substitute for tactical play, undermining the stated "wins through positioning, not reflexes" pillar; also flagged the Mana-regen drain floor (2/sec) as unreachable dead code given the hard 2-application stacking cap (5 − 1.5×2 = 2 exactly).
- **Narrative critic:** MAJOR — the mid-fight pay-to-heal fee and the Debuffer archetype both ship with zero fictional framing (no lore hook, no explanation of what spending coin to heal mid-boss-fight means diegetically), reading as a bare monetization-style toggle bolted onto tactical combat.
- **Player psychologist:** BLOCKING — the recovery fee scales with Hexcoin *earned this expedition*, so players who performed well can easily afford it while players actually spiraling toward death (low HP, likely low Hexcoin) can least afford the mechanic meant to save them — inverting the stated purpose of preventing an "unavoidable death spiral."
- **Feasibility lead:** BLOCKING — the fee's basis (expedition-scoped vs. lifetime Hexcoin) is explicitly unresolved in both templates ("Do not implement against either reading until confirmed"), so this bundle cannot actually be built as-is despite being dispatched to close the top BLOCKING finding.
- **Adversarial QA:** MAJOR — no cap on total phase-recovery purchases within a single multi-phase boss fight (a 5-phase boss could recover up to ~60% of the pool across 4 breaks), and "computed fresh at the moment of that phase-break" leaves undefined whether mid-fight kills (minions/adds) feed the "earned this expedition" total, opening a possible self-funding fee loophole.
- **Business analyst:** BLOCKING — two Hexcoin sinks (the existing flat 100-fee spell-choice and the new 50%-of-earnings recovery fee) now draw on the same expedition-scoped pool whose reachability/farmability is *already* an open, escalated disagreement per `hexcoin-template.md`; the economy cannot be tuned or evaluated until that's resolved, and there's no stated cap preventing Hexcoin-rich players from systematically buying their way around the death-trigger Mastery-loss penalty across a whole run.

No Docker available in this environment; would have run `typecheck`/`build` per the docker-testing-contract if it were — flagging that substitution rather than letting it pass silently, per the contract's own instruction.

**BLOCKING**

1. **Systems designer — the wall-slide fix gets the sign backwards for the axis it wasn't specifically designed around.** `Enemy.ts`'s retreat-blocked branch picks a slide direction via `towardCenter = this.y >= this.lane.centerY ? -1 : 1`, then `velocity = perpendicular * towardCenter`. This is correctly tuned for the case Warden's spec actually names — pinning against the lane's *short ends* (left/right, `blockedRight`/`blockedLeft`) — I hand-verified all four left/right sign combinations and the enemy always slides toward the vertical centerline as intended. But the code applies the identical `towardCenter`-by-*y*-position logic when `blockedBottom`/`blockedTop` (the *long* walls) trigger instead, and there the correct escape direction depends on `direction.x`'s sign (the mage's horizontal offset), not the enemy's own y-position — a variable the formula never looks at. Concrete counterexample: enemy at (400, 300) near the bottom wall, mage at (600, 200) (up-and-right) → `direction≈(0.894,-0.447)`, `perpendicular≈(-0.447,-0.894)`, `towardCenter=-1` (y=300≥270) → final velocity ≈ `(0.447, 0.894)·speed`. The y-component is *positive* — the enemy is commanded to move further down, deeper into the wall it's already pinned against, the exact nose-pinning behavior this fix was built to eliminate. This isn't a rare corner case: `RANGED_PREFERRED_RANGE` (240) and `DEBUFFER_PREFERRED_RANGE` (150) both dwarf the lane's own ±80px vertical half-span (`ROAD_HEIGHT` 160), so a kiting enemy sitting near a vertical extreme is a routine outcome, not an edge case, and roughly half the time (whichever side of the enemy the mage happens to be on) the "fix" makes the pin worse instead of resolving it. Separately verified (so this isn't reflexively "everything's broken"): true two-wall corner blocks (e.g. `blockedRight`+`blockedBottom` together) are structurally self-resolving under this same formula — I derived all four corner sign combinations by hand and in each, exactly one axis escapes regardless of which sign `towardCenter` picks, so corners are fine. The bug is specific and isolated to the single-axis top/bottom trigger.

2. **Systems designer / Adversarial QA — the Mastery growth-rate sizing (backlog 0.4) is built on a premise the game's own shipped numbers already contradict.** `mastery-template.md` and `pato/log.md` (2026-07-25 (3)) both derive the 20-casts/tier number from "a 1-target spell can accrue up to ~1 landed cast per enemy killed" — i.e., 1 landed cast ≈ 1 kill. That's false against the numbers already in the repo: `Enemy.ts`'s placeholder HP (melee 18, ranged 14, debuffer 22) is 2-6x every Novice-tier 1-target spell's power (`stone_spike` 4, `magma_lance` 9) — no spell one-shots any enemy at Novice tier, so every kill costs multiple landed casts, not one. Recomputed against Level 2's actual, file-verified composition (melee 8 / ranged 7 / debuffer 6, 21 total): spamming `stone_spike` (power 4, the fastest-mastering spell that's actually default-equipped and reachable, see finding 4) exclusively yields `8×5 + 7×4 + 6×6 = 104` landed casts available in that single level alone — 2.6x the 40 casts needed for full Novice→Master, not "nearly two full levels" as the template states. `magma_lance` (power 9, the hardest 1-target hitter, not default-equipped) still yields 48 in Level 2 alone — still over the 40-cast full-mastery threshold, inside one level. The "ordinary varied play" side of the same derivation (~20.5 casts across all 3 levels, meant to show generalist play stays far short) carries the identical flaw — it also assumes a landed cast permanently clears `base_targets` enemies rather than needing several hits per kill. Both halves of the claimed asymmetry ("only actively-mained spells progress... most of the kit stays Novice/Adept") are therefore wrong in the same direction: real achievable landed-cast counts are dramatically higher than modeled, for both specialist and generalist play, which is exactly the failure mode ("caps most of the spellbook at Master within a single level") backlog 0.4 was created to avoid. The rate may still turn out fine, but the number wasn't actually checked against the numbers that exist in the same codebase.

**MAJOR**

3. **Player psychologist — the aiming fallback's permanent switch defeats the exact user it was built for.** `SpellroadScene.ts`'s `pointerHasMoved` flips true on the *first* `pointermove` event and never resets — there's no path back to last-move-direction aiming for the rest of the session. This flag was added specifically because of a "trackpad-difficulty complaint" (`ana/log.md` 2026-07-25 (2)), but a trackpad is exactly the input surface most prone to firing incidental `pointermove` events (a resting or twitching finger, not a deliberate aim) — meaning the player this fallback exists for is likely to trip the permanent mouse-only switch within seconds of starting, with no way back to the movement-based aiming they may have actually wanted to keep using.

4. **Feasibility lead — the default 6-spell hotbar loadout is an accident of file order, not a designed kit, and it orphans half the just-shipped content.** `equippedSpells = this.spells.slice(0, 6)` resolves to `arc_lance`/`flame_sweep`/`frost_nova` (originally shipped) plus `stone_spike`/`flare_jab`/`spark_ring` — the first 3 of Frieren's new 9, which are exactly that batch's 3 Light-weight entries, purely because of how the 3×3 grid happened to be enumerated in the log.
